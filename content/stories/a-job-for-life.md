"What, exactly, does the job entail, then?", Ralph asked. The interview had been going well and he was beginning to think he might be in with a chance.

"Well, the post is for a Programme Development Officer, you saw the advert." The chairman of the interviewing panel was not being very helpful. His sidekick was making agitated little movements with his hands, crossing and uncrossing his fingers, palms pressed together. He had not done much during the interview, other than mop his sweating bald head with an oversized handkerchief, push his reading glasses on to the bridge of his nose with his forefinger, and anxiously moving his pen around his pad. He had several documents piled up in front of him. Ralph wondered whether this sidekick was more nervous than he was and resented him for this apparent intrusion on his own feelings of apprehension.
"Mr Dreyfus, you must have read the job description that we would have sent you. You must know what job you've applied for, surely?"

Ralph had not warmed to this sidekick, but now that he had spoken in his thin, high-pitched, wheedley voice, Ralph liked him even less. "I did receive it, yes, but I am afraid that it was couched in words that conveyed very little. What do you actually do here?".

"Our department produces reports on potential developments in the organization’s procedures. We have to evaluate and benchmark all potential innovations and make recommendations to management. And, like everyone else, we are subjected to performance evaluation through targets, to ensure that we continuously improve our productivity." Ralph was confused. To him, productivity referred to making things more efficiently but it appeared that these people made nothing. They only reported on the activities of others, and in turn had to report to more managers above them. “What does the organization actually sell?” he asked. The question was met with blank expressions.
